Methodology

Every number on this map is computed, sourced, and bounded. This page says how — and just as deliberately, what each figure can not claim.

1 · What the map shows

Each dot is a grid node with published demand (offtake) connection headroom — the load a new connection could draw there today, as stated by the operator's own capacity publication. Generation (injection) capacity is a different quantity and only ever appears as the separately-labelled grid-strength proxy layer. Purple = queued connection requests (capacity already claimed — the opposite of headroom). Teal = operator-flagged viable, no figure published. Coverage: 10 markets, assembled operator by operator — regulator → licensed-operator registry → capacity publication — normalised into one schema.

Headroom is non-additive: nearby nodes share upstream limits. Never sum two dots into one bigger site.

2 · The firm-power engine (the uptime slider)

The headline tiles come from a chronological battery simulation on a full year of measured system load per market (15/30-minute resolution). A candidate load T draws continuously through the connection; a battery of duration D (energy = T×D, ~88% round-trip) discharges through every interval in which system load + T would exceed the year's observed peak, and may recharge only when spare room exists under that peak — through the same connection. Event length and clustering are therefore fully priced: back-to-back constraint days with thin recharge windows are exactly where the simulation fails a candidate load.

Uptime means the share of intervals in which the load runs at full T. Any shortfall, however small, counts the whole interval as downtime — the SLA view. 99.9% uptime ≈ 8.8 hours of curtailment per year. The slider moves the required uptime; every tile recomputes from precomputed simulation results.

The capability ceiling is the observed single-year system peak — a deliberate floor. Sensitivity: allowing just 3% above the observed peak roughly triples the 2h figure; a defensible per-market capability derivation from adequacy reports is on the roadmap. These are system-level screening numbers: local network constraints still gate any specific node.

3 · Flexible-load headroom (no battery)

The "flexible load" tile follows the Nicholas Institute's Rethinking Load Growth method (Norris et al., 2025): the maximum constant load addable against the observed system peak such that the energy it must shed stays within a tolerance (shown at ≤1% of its annual energy). Note the metric difference: this is an energy tolerance for a load that itself flexes; the battery tiles use uptime, which is stricter.

4 · The fit score (Neura funnel)

Each demand node is scored 0–100 for the firming wedge at your chosen target load: 45% firming-gap economics (sweet spot where published headroom covers most-but-not-all of the target, so a right-sized battery bridges the rest), 20% voltage class, 15% industrial land within 3 km, 20% queued-demand pressure within 30 km where the market publishes a queue. Tiers: prime ≥78 · strong 63–77 · possible 48–62 · weak <48. The score ranks screening effort — it is not a bankability rating.

5 · Published vs measured

Published headroom is an operator statement, not a measurement. Where substation-level metered load exists (currently Portugal's E-Redes; Great Britain at ~4,000 nodes pending API keys), we compute the real load-duration curve and the empirically firmable added load — rows and popups marked measured. The two can disagree in both directions; where a published figure exceeds measured minimum headroom, treat the published number as the optimistic bound.

6 · Solar & BESS assets (GB)

Asset dots join the government planning database (REPD — coordinates, status, operational year) with the NESO TEC register (connection site, queue gate). Pipeline assets are shown as potential capacity; operational assets as installed capacity. Battery duration (MWh) is not published in either source and is marked accordingly until the DNO register join lands.

7 · Candidate sites

Industrial and warehouse land (OSM polygons) within 3 km of a ≥100 MW transmission node. Contracted power per building is not publicly screenable anywhere in Europe — sites are a shortlist to verify through meter-level records, never confirmed availability.

8 · Sources & freshness

Node capacity: national operator publications per market (editions shown in each popup and the freshness strip). System load: ENTSO-E-derived hourly series (Fraunhofer energy-charts mirror) and NESO historic demand for GB, full calendar year. Assets: REPD (DESNZ) × NESO TEC. Measured profiles: E-Redes open data. Network-physics work in progress is calibrated against operator-published PTDFs and static grid models (JAO Core/Nordic, NESO ETYS).

9 · What none of this is

Screening, not a connection study. System-level firm figures are not nodal promises; nodal headroom is not additive; a fit score is not a bankability opinion; single-weather-year results move with the weather. Every deal this surfaces still ends in a formal access request and site-level verification — this map exists to make sure you spend that effort in the right places.
