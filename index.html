Reading the map
550100250500900+

Bubble area scales with headroom. White ring = 400 kV substation, grey = lower voltage. Positions snap to the matched town or substation centroid — indicative. Transmission nodes carry ≥100 MW; distribution tops out around 60–140 MW.

Every dot is offtake headroom — the load a new connection (a datacenter, an electrolyser, a factory) could draw at that point today. Injection capacity for generators is a different market and only appears as the explicitly-labelled grid-strength proxy layer. Purple dots are queued connection requests: demand already claimed, the opposite of headroom. Teal dots are points an operator has flagged viable without publishing a figure.

Headroom is shared. A large load at one node consumes its neighbours' margin too — two nearby dots never add up to one bigger site.

Ranges become floors. Where operators publish bands instead of numbers (Svenska kraftnät, several Finnish DSOs), the map carries the band's lower bound so it never overstates.

Lines are a proxy. No European TSO or DSO publishes live line loadings; line colouring shows the strongest node headroom within 12 km. Spanish transmission circuits additionally carry a published thermal rating (MVA) — a design ceiling, not spare capacity.

Sites are a shortlist. Contracted power per building is only verifiable through meter-level records (DSO enquiry) — the site finder surfaces targets to verify, not confirmed availability.

How it's assembled: there is no pan-European source. Coverage is built market by market — energy regulator → licensed-operator registry → each operator's capacity publication — then normalised into a single schema and served as a static map.
