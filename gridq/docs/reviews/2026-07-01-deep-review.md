### dispatch-milp

The dispatch layer is a two-seam design: the inner `DispatchSolver` Protocol (src/gridq/dispatch/contract.py — `DispatchProblem` frozen dataclass in, contract-valid `DispatchResult` pydantic model out, must never raise on insufficient data) and the production `MilpDispatchSolver` (src/gridq/optimisation/milp_solver.py, MODEL_VERSION "highs-milp-v1") which solves a deterministic half-hourly battery dispatch for ONE fixed spec against the site's own metered load and time-of-use tariff. Formulation per half-hour t (Δt = interval/60 h): continuous variables charge_t, discharge_t (kW AC, lower=0) and soe_t (kWh, 0..energy); cyclic SoE balance `soe_t − soe_{t-1} − charge_t·RTE·Δt + discharge_t·Δt = 0` (RTE = round_trip_efficiency.ac_round_trip, applied on charge so grid charge = discharge ÷ RTE); variable upper bounds encode connection and BTM limits: charge_ub = min(power, max(0, min(MIC,ASC) − site_load_t)), discharge_ub = min(power, site_load_t) — strictly no export; one dense annual warranty-throughput constraint `Σ discharge_t·Δt ≤ cap_cycles·energy/annual_factor` from warranty_cycle_limits.max_cycles_per_year_by_duration_h. Objective: maximise `Σ price_t·(discharge_t − charge_t)·Δt − 1e-7·Σ(charge+discharge)·Δt` where price_t = site TOU energy rate (import_rates via tariff_calendar band→clock-window mapping) + DUoS red/amber/green rate; the ε term is a tie-break only. Solved with linopy → HiGHS, single-threaded, fixed seed, presolve on, IPM + crossover (deterministic; ~2.5s for a full metered year). Despite the name it contains NO integer variables — it is an LP; simultaneous charge/discharge is avoided by the crossover-to-vertex + ε regulariser, not a binary.

**Gaps (for multi-market + firming):**
- Perfect foresight, confirmed: the MILP optimises against the actual metered half-hourly load and a deterministic tariff calendar over the historic year with full knowledge of every interval (cyclic SoE) — no forecast error, no receding horizon, no stochastic/robust formulation; DispatchProblem.scenario exists but is Any and unused by the solver (scenario=None everywhere in engine.py)
- No reserved headroom / SoC floors for committed availability windows: soe bounds are a flat [0, energy]; there is no mechanism to hold energy or power headroom during CM stress events or awarded flex tender windows — CM and Piclo revenue are valued post-hoc in valuation.py on a schedule that never reserved capability for them (schedule.tender_window_discharge_mwh and deliverable_capacity_mw are optional fields that nothing currently sets)
- No simultaneous multi-service allocation: charge/discharge are single undifferentiated flows; no per-service power split variables, no mutual-exclusivity or capacity-sharing constraints across streams — only two tariff streams are co-optimised, everything else is priced off the same schedule afterwards
- No cycle/degradation cost in the objective and no degradation state: warranty throughput is a hard cap only; docs/dispatch_engine.md §2.2 explicitly says a degradation marginal-cost term and parasitic load are omitted in v1; no multi-year capability decay, so CM final-year deliverability (D12) is unverified
- No export variable at all: the formulation cannot express export revenue, export limits (the contract docstring names an export-limit descriptor but the solver never builds one), or wholesale/BM/ancillary dispatch — adding them requires new decision variables and a price series shape that is itself deferred (D20)
- No penalty/availability terms: no unavailability penalty, no delivery-failure cost, no firming/imbalance modelling — nothing to price the risk of committing capacity
- problem.constraints (the DispatchConstraintDescriptor union from dispatch_constraints_for) is accepted on the seam but the MILP never reads it — MilpDispatchSolver resolves MIC/ASC and warranty caps directly from site/assumptions, so stream-declared constraints do not actually shape dispatch
- No demand-charge / peak-shave (kVA/capacity-charge) term: import-peak reduction is not in the objective (MIC/ASC headroom is deliberately £0 and there is no max-import decision variable), so a capacity-charge saving cannot be priced
- GB-hardcoded time windows: red-band and TOU windows are code constants in tariff_calendar.py, not versioned assumption content — a second market (or a non-post-TCR DNO) needs the promised assumption-version override that does not exist yet
- Single-year, single-scenario dispatch: no scenario-set dispatch (P50/P90 differentiation happens in finance/scenarios, not in dispatch), no multi-year horizon for degradation-aware sizing

**Notable:**
- It is an LP wearing a MILP name: no binary/integer variables anywhere (MODEL_VERSION 'highs-milp-v1'); no-simultaneous-charge/discharge is guaranteed only by the ε tie-break plus IPM crossover to a vertex solution — adding true multi-service commitment or availability windows will likely need real binaries and change solve-time characteristics (the doc records dual simplex was already pathologically slow, ~15s, on the one dense throughput constraint)
- Half-built multi-market seam: HalfHourlyDispatchSchedule already has deliverable_capacity_mw and tender_window_discharge_mwh fields, and DispatchProblem has scenario + constraints, but nothing populates or consumes them — the seams for CM deliverability and flex windows exist in the data model but not in the formulation
- Chosen candidate is dispatched twice: MilpEngine._dispatch_energy_for re-solves the selected spec after enumerate_sizing to recover volumes and the last_schedule() for the dispatch chart — cheap now (~2.5s/solve) but a doubling that matters if the formulation grows
- Cyclic SoE (soe.roll(t=1)) means the first interval borrows from the last — fine for an annual horizon, but a subtle wrap-around artefact for short/partial horizons
- Value attribution asymmetry: arbitrage_value_gbp and duos_avoidance_value_gbp are floored at max(0, ·) per stream, so a schedule that sacrifices one price component for the other could under-report the negative side while the combined objective was optimal
- CM value uses schedule.power_mw × de-rating — the nameplate spec, not anything the dispatch proved deliverable; the 'deliverability unverified' warning is carried in the stream notes
- The doc's 'Still deferred' list (docs/dispatch_engine.md §7) is an honest forward register: Modo wholesale price series shape (D20), calendar-as-assumption, D13 promotion, CM final-year horizon (D12), warm-start/parallel solves, shadow-price valuation of MIC/ASC headroom

### finance-scenarios

The finance package (src/gridq/finance/) is M5: a fully deterministic site-level financial model that turns SiteModel + any ResultLike OptimisationResult + a versioned AssumptionLibrary into a FinancialCase. build_cashflow_model (cashflows.py) prices only value streams with firmness in {FIRM, CONTRACTED} (_PRICED_FIRMNESS; conditional/gated-upside held out), estimates capex from battery_capex.installed_cost, builds 7 named cost lines (COST_LINE_KEYS) each from one library assumption — levies_on_charge stays None until a DispatchEnergy hook supplies charge volume — and produces P50/P90 EBITDA and pre-debt FCF series over a horizon (default DEFAULT_HORIZON_YEARS=15, always warned). P90 is NOT a scenario/probabilistic construct: it is P50 revenue × (1 − p50_p90_haircuts.p90_revenue_haircut_pct/100) per year, with degradation.annual_capacity_fade_pct compounding decay applied to revenue only (costs flat, no indexation, no tax/working capital). run_financial_model (financial_model.py) then chains debt/DSCR (P90 CFADS, level-payment annuity), P50 payback + project IRR (bisection, one-sign-change gate), levered equity_irr net of the same debt service, and recommend_price, stamping a RunContext whose run_id derives from a timestamp-free input hash. DSCR is 'unavailable' in v1 by design: the M9 credit mapping (credit.py) resolves rate/tenor from CreditStatus via financing.credit_rate_tenor_policy, but gearing (financing.debt_gearing_pct, Sim's portfolio finance) is absent from gb_v0_1, so DebtTerms never assembles, min_dscr=None, covenant passes=None, and every case is not bankable.

The scenarios package (src/gridq/scenarios/) is M6: discrete Low/Central/High evaluation, no Monte Carlo. ScenarioEngine.run applies each Scenario's cost_multiplier to year-0 capex and price_multiplier to all operating-year net cashflows of the base FinancialCase (a first-order net-cashflow scaling, not a revenue/cost split), shifts whole-year COD delays (sub-annual warned, unshifted), and produces a probability-weighted summary — weights come from the assumption library key scenario_weights.central_low_high and are normalised with warnings if off-sum (weights.py). Low/High multipliers are never derived: absent bands default to identity 1.0 + a ranked-data-ask warning (decision D20). External price forecasts enter only as hash-pinned ForecastReference objects (content_hash 'sha256:<64hex>' recomputed from the curve, verified flag set only from real files), and forecast_prices.scenario_price_series resolves per-band £/MWh curves for the dispatch engine with drift detection. Scenario runs can never be bankable_candidate; unverified forecast refs force NOT_BANKABLE.

**Gaps (for multi-market + firming):**
- No availability-payment revenue shape: revenue is a single annual £ per stream from ValueStreamResult.annual_revenue_gbp; nothing models capacity/availability payments with availability tests, or £/kW/yr structures (capacity_fee pricing is a structure-only placeholder)
- No penalty / liquidated-damages modelling anywhere: DSCR downside is purely the flat p90_revenue_haircut_pct; no underperformance LDs, availability penalties, or CM non-delivery charges
- P90 is a single flat percentage haircut on total revenue (p50_p90_haircuts.p90_revenue_haircut_pct), identical across streams and years — no per-stream firmness-differentiated haircuts, no contracted-vs-merchant distinction in the P90 construction
- No contracted-vs-merchant split in debt sizing: debt is sized as capex x gearing on total P90 CFADS; a lender-style structure (size debt only against contracted CFADS, merchant as equity upside) has no seam; DSCR-sculpting to a target is documented future work (level annuity only)
- Multi-year revenue decay is a single compounding capacity-fade proxy applied uniformly to all streams — no per-stream firmness decay (the market-interface 'firmness & decay' concept doesn't reach cashflows), no year-by-year throughput/degradation from dispatch, no re-contracting cliffs (e.g. CM contract end, PPA expiry mid-horizon), no cost indexation
- Gearing absent (financing.debt_gearing_pct, Sim's) => DSCR, covenant verdict, equity IRR all unavailable on the default path; the sizing MILP's 'P90-DSCR constraint inside the objective' has no live DSCR to constrain against
- Fixed £/kWh price-to-target requires annual delivered-energy kWh via the DispatchEnergy hook — OptimisationResult still doesn't carry charge/discharge volumes, so levies_on_charge and the price recommendation stay unavailable on the pipeline path
- Multi-market: scenario band mapping (_BAND_ALIASES) only knows low/central/base/high labels (Modo labels unconfirmed, C012); price_unit is a free string with no FX/unit conversion; single-currency GBP throughout field names (annual_revenue_by_stream_gbp etc.)
- Scenario price_multiplier scales net operating cashflow (revenue minus all costs), not revenue — a price shock therefore also scales fixed opex; honest-coarse by design but wrong shape for firming analysis where costs are largely price-insensitive
- No debt refinancing, DSRA/reserve accounts, tax, or working capital; horizon shorter than tenor only warns and measures DSCR on overlapping years
- Monte Carlo / correlated stochastic scenarios deferred to portfolio work behind the ScenarioSet interface — v1 has exactly 3 discrete cases with library weights

**Notable:**
- Two disjoint 'scenario' concepts coexist: build_cashflow_model reads a duck-typed scenario (price_multiplier scales year-1 revenue, cost_multiplier scales capex) INSIDE the finance model, while ScenarioEngine separately re-scales the finished FinancialCase cashflows from outside. Same multiplier names, different application points — the finance path scales revenue only; the M6 path scales net operating cashflow. A caller composing both would double-apply.
- P50 vs P90 in finance is purely the haircut assumption; P50 vs P90 in ScenarioResult are both scaled by the SAME scenario multipliers — Low/Central/High is an orthogonal axis to P50/P90, not a probabilistic refinement of it.
- debt.py carries a legacy flat-key path (build_debt_terms reading financing.debt_gearing_pct/debt_interest_rate_pct/debt_tenor_years) that always returns None on gb_v0_1, alongside the live credit-driven path (build_debt_terms_from_credit) — assess_debt still calls the legacy path when no terms are injected, but run_financial_model pre-resolves via credit first, so the legacy reader only fires when the credit mapping fails. Half-built seam.
- DebtAssumptions on the FinancialCase can carry rate_pct/tenor_years with gearing_pct=None — the M9 mapping is deliberately made visible on the case even though it can't size debt yet.
- equity_irr docstring hard-codes the discipline that principal/debt-service must come off the DscrResult (drift guard between DSCR and levered return) — a contract enforced only by convention, not types.
- The financing section accretes keys from three owners: credit_rate_tenor_policy (M9/Sim policy), debt_gearing_pct (Sim, absent), target_equity_irr_pct (pricing) — the gearing key's arrival is the single switch that activates DSCR, covenant, and equity IRR with zero code change.
- verify_forecast_reference and scenario_price_series mutate ref.verified in place on a pydantic model — the only intentionally-mutable provenance flag in an otherwise frozen-shape system.
- ScenarioEngine warns but still applies cost_multiplier when cashflows[0] > 0 (no capex outflow) — the year-0-is-capex convention is assumed, not enforced.
- graphify hook fires on every Read but the CLI is not installed on this machine, per the orchestrator note — ignored as instructed.

### quotes-outputs-app

This subsystem is the run-to-artifact spine: `generate_quote` (src/gridq/quotes/pipeline.py) chains a graded SiteModel through a dependency-injected `OptimisationEngine` (default `BaselineEngine`; the app injects the real `MilpEngine`) → `run_financial_model` → `assemble_quote_record` → `emit_quote_telemetry`, returning a `QuoteRunResult` (quote, telemetry, site, result, financial_case, bankable, sizing_grid, dispatch_profile). The `QuoteRecord` (src/gridq/schemas/quote.py) is stamped with a deterministic `quote_id` derived from a timestamp-free canonical hash (site + result + financial_case + assumption_version + forecast content_hash), plus flat input_hash/assumption_version/code_version; two additive, hash-excluded fields carry Wave-5 chart data — `sizing_grid` (full power×duration grid with levered_return_pct / min_p90_dscr / passes_covenant per point, read from the engine's duck-typed `last_sizing_run()` hook) and `dispatch_profile` (one deterministically-chosen representative half-hourly day, `last_dispatch_profile()` hook). Lifecycle is a strict forward state machine (src/gridq/quotes/lifecycle.py): draft → indicative → underwritten → contracted → operating, lapse from any live state; transitions re-validate via `model_validate` so schema guards (underwritten-from-T0 needs a warning) re-fire.

Outputs (M8, src/gridq/outputs/) render three consistent-by-construction artifacts from one `OutputViewModel` (viewmodel.py, the only reader of the record): the capital-facing underwriting memo (bankability banner, system spec, P50/P90 cashflow table with "P90 is the bankable basis" note, DSCR+covenant+sensitivities slot, contracted-vs-merchant split where floor = firmness∈{firm,contracted} and all `gated_upside` is merchant, price-to-target, debt, counterparty, consents/gates, provenance, warnings), the customer proposal (secured floor vs upside kept out of the headline; bill before/after is a labelled-pending seam — no bill model in v1), and a self-contained HTML report. Hard rules are test-enforced: no arithmetic in html_report (AST scan), no invented numbers (None → `formatting.unavailable(reason)`), prose drafter is categorical-only and LLM-free by default, byte-identical regeneration. The FastAPI app (src/gridq/app/app.py) is a server-rendered product surface: wizard intake (with synthetic HH profile generation + reference tariff defaults, both disclosed via warnings and never bankable), background run jobs polled via /api/run/{job_id}, per-quote screens (inspect with 3D sizing surface / dispatch chart / utilisation / value-stack islands built in view.py, spec, artifacts, telemetry), and a file-per-run gitignored store keyed by deterministic quote_id (store.py).

**Gaps (for multi-market + firming):**
- No firming-commitment representation in outputs: StreamRow carries only stream/annual_revenue_gbp/firmness/gate_status/unlock_condition — there is no field for contract tenor, counterparty of the firming contract, firmness decay over years, or the committed volume/window a lender would underwrite; the memo's split section can only say 'contracted' without saying contracted-with-whom-for-how-long
- No availability-window concept anywhere in the quote/outputs/UI: DispatchProfilePoint has DUoS red_band but no market-availability window (e.g. CM stress events, Piclo service windows, ancillary availability blocks); the dispatch chart cannot show 'reserved for service X 17:00-19:00' and utilisation_island's modes are charging/peak-discharge/off-peak-discharge/idle only — no 'held available' state, which is what a firmed multi-market battery mostly does
- DispatchProfile is GB-shaped, not market-agnostic: duos_rate_gbp_per_kwh and red_band are required fields of the frozen-ish schema (src/gridq/schemas/dispatch_profile.py); a second market's dispatch day cannot be persisted without either schema growth or violating the 'core never branches on market' principle
- Single-day dispatch legibility only: only one representative day survives to the QuoteRecord (dispatch_schedule_ref is a content hash, the full schedule is dropped at the seam), so seasonal availability, cycling budget consumption, and worst-day firming headroom cannot be shown to a customer or lender from saved data
- No per-stream dispatch attribution: the dispatch chart shows aggregate charge/discharge but not which value stream each half-hour serves — multi-market stacking (e.g. CM availability + DUoS avoidance + Piclo) is not decomposable in any artifact
- Landing stats hardcode "markets": 1 (app.py landing route); the app has no market/region selector, ASSUMPTION_VERSION is pinned to the single constant 'gb_v0_1', and the run store has no market dimension
- Sensitivities slot is plumbed but never fed: generate_artifacts accepts SensitivityRow tuples 'e.g. from the scenario engine (M6)' but no caller (CLI or app) passes scenario-engine output, so the Low/Central/High DSCR grid always renders labelled-pending
- TelemetryEvent has no realised-vs-underwritten fields despite the architecture principle naming them — only quote-creation-time metrics (human_minutes, iterations, missing_data_count); no event is emitted on lifecycle transitions, so time-to-quote/outcome derivation has no rows to derive from, and nothing in the app calls transition_quote at all (quotes are born INDICATIVE and stay there)
- Customer bill before/after (proposal headline) still pending on the M5 bill model (C017); counterparty/consents/gates need the SiteModel passed separately because QuoteRecord embeds only site_id (C018) — a portable quote.json alone cannot render lender-complete artifacts
- No firming/availability data asks: build_ranked_data_asks surfaces missing site inputs, but there is no ask category for export consent, RTM contract status, or availability-window confirmation that a firmed multi-market quote would need

**Notable:**
- The engine seam leaks additively via duck typing: three getattr-based hooks (selected_dispatch_energy, last_sizing_run, last_dispatch_profile) in pipeline.py are the sanctioned escape hatch around the frozen OptimisationResult — any new multi-market/firming artifact (availability schedule, per-stream allocation) will likely arrive as hook #4/#5, and this pattern is stateful (last_*) so it assumes one solve per engine instance
- Tension with the market-agnostic principle: DispatchProfilePoint hardcodes GB concepts (duos_rate_gbp_per_kwh, red_band) as required schema fields, and view.py/utilisation_island label modes 'red-band peak shaving and DUoS avoidance' — the presentation layer branches on GB content even though the core does not
- The lifecycle machine is fully built and tested but unused by the product surface: app.py creates every quote as QuoteMode.INDICATIVE and offers no transition UI; expiry_date exists on QuoteRecord but nothing sets or enforces it in the app
- _quote_complete() (app.py) is the app's own honesty gate on top of the pipeline's: a run that returns a quote but with no dispatch and no priceable grid point is treated as an error and never listed — half-runs are invisible in Projects
- The Theoretical Upside overlay (upside.py) deliberately breaks the 'every number from the record' rule at the UI layer only — ILLUSTRATIVE_BY_ID £/MW/yr constants are shown in the value stack keyed off a warning-string marker (UPSIDE_LABEL substring match), a fragile trigger mechanism
- Demo detection is also warning-string substring matching (DEMO_LABEL in quote.warnings) — warnings double as a de-facto tag/flag channel on the frozen QuoteRecord, suggesting a missing typed 'labels' field
- Reference tariffs and synthetic load profiles (_TARIFF_REF, gridq.app.synthetic) put estimate-grade numbers on the real pricing path (they build the SiteModel the MILP prices) — disclosed via warnings and tier-capped at T1, but they are the one place the app authors input numbers
- The dense 3D surface floor-fills None cells to the worst observed return before bilinear resampling (view._dense_surface) — a deliberate display-only fabrication, disclosed via holes_filled/z_floor, sitting right at the edge of the no-invented-numbers rule
- SizingGridPoint already carries end_of_life_deliverable (deliverability at final contract year) — a firming-relevant flag persisted per candidate but not yet surfaced in any artifact or chart tooltip beyond the schema

### sizing

The sizing subsystem (M4, src/gridq/sizing/) is a deterministic outer loop over the dispatch+finance contracts: `SearchSpace` (search_space.py) is a validated, sorted, de-duplicated power×duration cartesian grid — in production `MilpEngine._default_search_space` (src/gridq/optimisation/engine.py:232) builds it as POWER_FRACTIONS (0.25, 0.5, 1.0) of the site's binding scale (max_import_kw, else connection.mic_kw) × DEFAULT_DURATION_HOURS (1, 2, 4h), i.e. the current 3×3 = 9 candidates. `enumerate_sizing` (sizer.py) evaluates every candidate through the single per-spec primitive `_evaluate_spec`: build a `DispatchProblem`, solve full-year dispatch via any injected `DispatchSolver` (the real `MilpDispatchSolver` by default), map to the frozen `OptimisationResult`, then run `run_financial_model` with the dispatch-energy hook. `SizingObjective` (objective.py) is "maximise levered (equity) return s.t. P90 DSCR ≥ covenant": feasibility requires a known covenant (`dscr_covenants.min_p90_dscr` from the assumption library) AND an available min P90 DSCR AND not explicitly `end_of_life_deliverable is False`; ranking prefers `levered_return_pct` (FinancialCase.equity_irr, P50 basis, only when DebtTerms are injected), falls back to unlevered `project_irr_pct` (never bankable on the fallback), tie-breaking on smaller power then duration. P90 itself is NOT a distribution: finance/cashflows.py (~line 457) forms P90 EBITDA as P50 revenue × (1 − p50_p90_haircuts.p90_revenue_haircut_pct/100) with costs held flat and degradation decay already applied to revenue; debt.py sizes a level-payment annuity loan at gearing and measures DSCR = CFADS/debt-service year-by-year on that P90 CFADS (min over debt years = `min_p90_dscr`). `underwrite_spec` (underwrite.py) is the "one engine, called twice" second call — it independently reprices the chosen spec through the same `_evaluate_spec`, with `financial_fingerprint` (timestamp-free projection incl. p50/p90 cashflows, DSCR-by-year, input_hash) proving sizing and underwriting agree. `SizingRun.sizing_grid()` projects all evaluations into the frozen-schema `SizingGrid`/`SizingGridPoint` (src/gridq/schemas/sizing.py), persisted onto `QuoteRecord.sizing_grid` and rendered as the Wave-5 3D surface by the app. In flight on branch agent/sizing-search (uncommitted): stage 1 of issue 016 — `SizingDomain` (sizing/domain.py) replaces the 3×3 with a continuous 2-D power(kW)×energy(kWh) box where C-rate floats, duration = energy/power is banded to DEFAULT_DURATION_BAND_H (0.5–8.0h); deterministic `grid(n_power, n_energy)` (the dense exact-LP reference surface) and seeded `latin_hypercube(n, seed)` both emit ordinary `SizingCandidate`s so the same full-8760h LP inner evaluator is reused unchanged. ### value-stack

The value stack (src/gridq/value_stack/, ~1050 LOC + docs/value_stack.md) is the market-agnostic M2 layer: every revenue stream implements the `ValueStream` runtime-checkable Protocol (interface.py) — identity (`stream_id`/`display_name`), `required_inputs()` (drives the ranked data ask), `check_gates(site, assumptions, context)` → `GateResult`, `value_function(dispatch_schedule, site, assumptions, scenario, context)` → the frozen `ValueStreamResult`, and `constraints()` → `DispatchConstraintDescriptor` list. ## Part 2 — Analysis lenses (5 analysts)

### Lens: GB market reality — BTM battery as a firming solution against a flexible/volatile grid

GridQ's honest-floor architecture matches the GB mid-2026 commercial reality remarkably well in shape: the bankable revenue for a single-site BTM battery today genuinely is import-side (ToU/DUoS arbitrage, capacity-charge management), the merchant stack (wholesale/BM/ancillary) genuinely does require an aggregator route-to-market, and ancillary frequency response has saturated (prices down ~7x by 2024, now ~20-33% of BESS revenue per Timera/Modo), vindicating the repo's decision to keep DC/DM/DR as £0 gated upside. Three market facts sharpen this. ### Lens: architecture readiness — can the current abstractions carry firming products without breaking the rules?

The vocabulary is ready; the load-bearing seams are not. The generic ValueStream Protocol (src/gridq/value_stack/interface.py) genuinely names all six facets a firming product needs — value_function, constraints(), gate status→firmness mapping with triple-layer GATED_UPSIDE enforcement, GateContext (D12) for pre-solve facts, and constraint kinds 'availability_window' / 'held_state_of_energy' / 'deliverability' already declared on the CM/Piclo/ancillary streams (gb_streams.py:72-105). The additive-persistence pattern (duck-typed engine hooks → Optional, hash-excluded QuoteRecord fields, proven by sizing_grid/dispatch_profile in quotes/assembly.py:110-147) gives a safe path to persist an availability schedule without touching the frozen OptimisationResult. A ContractedStream subclass for a firming product can be written today and would classify, gate, and label itself correctly with zero core changes.

But a contracted availability product cannot actually be *expressed* today, for three verified reasons. First, the stream→dispatch constraint seam is decorative: dispatch_constraints_for() builds the descriptor union onto DispatchProblem.constraints (dispatch/contract.py:71,163-183), but grep confirms `problem.constraints` appears only in the contract docstring — MilpDispatchSolver resolves MIC/ASC and the warranty cap directly and builds exactly three constraint families (soe_balance, throughput_cap, variable bounds; milp_solver.py:229-244); descriptors carry no machine-readable parameters (constraint_id/description/kind/parameter_source strings only), so even a willing solver could not construct a reserved-SoE band or window mask from one. Second, nothing produces a GateContext (grep: only the class definition in interface.py:151), schedule.deliverable_capacity_mw and tender_window_discharge_mwh default None and are never set (optimisation/schedule.py:50-51), and end_of_life_deliverable is hardcoded None in sizer.py:285,301 — so CONTRACTED firmness is unreachable in production and deliverability is an unprovenance bare bool. Third, the revenue and finance shapes cannot carry an availability contract: ValueStreamResult (schemas/market.py) is a single annual £ with no availability-vs-utilisation split, no tenor, no penalty term (annual_cost_gbp is ge=0 and unused), and finance applies one flat P90 haircut regardless of firmness (finance/cashflows.py).

On the market-agnostic rule: the core interface stays clean, but three pressure points would crack under a new firming stream — the stream-id elif chain in optimisation/valuation.py:160-186 (engine-lane attribution requires editing the engine to add any stream), the hardcoded GB red/amber/green windows in tariff_calendar.py, and DispatchProfilePoint's required duos_rate_gbp_per_kwh/red_band fields (schemas/dispatch_profile.py:40-41). The honest split for the roadmap: GateContext growth, new stream classes, QuoteRecord additions, and engine hooks are additive; a new MarketStream enum member (schemas/enums.py:20-31), ValueStreamResult field growth, a new assumption section (AssumptionLibrary hard-codes 23 mandatory SECTION_NAMES with extra='forbid'), and any solver change that introduces binaries (the LP-not-MILP formulation, with its documented dual-simplex pathology) are deliberate frozen-contract / determinism-contract events. Verdict: firming products fit the architecture's shape without breaking the rules, but roughly half the work lands on frozen seams and the solver formulation, not on content.

### Lens: optimisation capability — MILP + sizer requirements for co-optimised multi-market + firming, composed with issue 016 adaptive sizing

The current engine is a clean, deterministic, honest single-service LP — not yet a multi-market optimiser. `MilpDispatchSolver` (src/gridq/optimisation/milp_solver.py) has exactly three continuous variable families (charge_t, discharge_t, soe_t; no binaries despite MODEL_VERSION "highs-milp-v1"), one objective (site bill reduction: TOU import rate + DUoS, minus an epsilon=1e-7 throughput tie-break), and four constraint families: cyclic SoE balance with RTE on charge, charge_ub = min(power, min(MIC,ASC) − load_t), discharge_ub = min(power, load_t) (structurally no export), and one dense annual warranty-throughput cap. It solves the actual metered year with perfect foresight (DispatchProblem.scenario is passed but engine.py:219 hardcodes scenario=None; the solver never reads it), and — crucially — `problem.constraints` (the DispatchConstraintDescriptor list from `dispatch_constraints_for`) is accepted on the seam but never read inside `_solve_milp`: the value stack's declared firming constraints (`_CM_HELD_SOE` kind 'held_state_of_energy', `_TENDER_WINDOW`/`_ANCILLARY_AVAILABILITY` kind 'availability_window', export limit — all in src/gridq/value_stack/gb_streams.py) exist as names only. Consequently CM and Piclo £ are attributed post-hoc in src/gridq/optimisation/valuation.py against a schedule that never reserved a kW or kWh for them — the same energy is implicitly sold twice (arbitrage in the objective, availability in the attribution), and `HalfHourlyDispatchSchedule.deliverable_capacity_mw`/`tender_window_discharge_mwh` (schedule.py) are never populated. On the sizing side, the deliverability feasibility constraint the objective advertises is a stub: `_evaluate_spec` always sets `end_of_life_deliverable=None` with a data-ask (src/gridq/sizing/sizer.py:214,285,301), so `SizingObjective.feasible` (objective.py:82) can only reject a False that nothing ever produces — "size day-one for end-of-tenor capability" is doc prose, not code.

The single most severe honesty gap for this lens is the perfect-foresight premium: the library carries `p50_p90_haircuts.perfect_foresight_haircut_pct` = 20% (assumptions/gb_v0_1.yaml:132, owner Olly, needs_review, "must be calibrated/versioned (M7)"), but grep confirms it has zero consumers in src/gridq/finance/ and src/gridq/optimisation/ — finance applies only `p90_revenue_haircut_pct` (cashflows.py:460). P50 revenue therefore equals the raw perfect-foresight LP optimum, an upper bound no real EMS achieves, and there is no M7 package anywhere under src/gridq/ (only the app/dev/etc. directories exist). M7's real job is now well-defined by the machinery around it: implement a causal (receding-horizon or rule-based) dispatch policy over the same metered year, measure its value ratio to the exact LP per stream and per site shape, and version that ratio into the assumption library as the calibrated haircut — plus, once sites operate, close the already-instrumented realised-vs-underwritten telemetry loop. Until then, at minimum the existing 20% placeholder should be consumed and labelled rather than ignored.

Firming composes awkwardly but tractably with issue 016 (docs/issues/016-adaptive-sizing-search.md, stage 1 of 9 built: `SizingDomain` in src/gridq/sizing/domain.py, no caller yet — `enumerate_sizing` still takes only `SearchSpace`). Two collisions must be resolved before stages 3–5: (1) duration-keyed assumptions (`warranty_cycle_limits.max_cycles_per_year_by_duration_h`, `capacity_market.de_rating_factor_by_duration_h`, keys {1,2,4}h with conservative flooring via `value_for_duration`) make the objective piecewise-constant with step discontinuities exactly where the continuous C-rate domain floats — a surrogate refiner assuming smoothness will mislocate optima at those steps, and issue 016 does not yet address interpolation; (2) firm commitments turn sizing into a constrained problem whose feasible region (derated capability at final contract year ≥ committed MW, i.e. power × derate(duration) × (1−fade)^N ≥ C) carves the 2-D box — the refiner must be feasibility-aware, not just objective-driven, and the dense exact-grid reference (the 016 certification instrument) is the right safety net for both. Computationally the plan holds if the inner problem stays an LP: ~2.5s per full-year solve single-threaded (docs/dispatch_engine.md §3 benchmark), so a ~1,600-point dense grid is ~67 CPU-minutes, embarrassingly parallel (016 stage 4). The risks are additive: true availability-commitment binaries or export under penalty terms convert the LP to a genuine MILP (the doc already records dual simplex going pathological at ~15s on one dense constraint), and any per-year re-dispatch over a 15-year tenor multiplies the budget ~15x — so multi-year deliverability should be an analytic capability check per candidate (closed-form derate × fade), not a multi-year solve, for as long as possible.

### Lens: underwriting & bankability — firming revenue in the priced floor, lender-grade pack

The honesty scaffolding is genuinely lender-grade in structure: the bankability gate is conjunctive (src/gridq/finance/financial_model.py:150-155 — cashflows bankable AND DSCR available AND covenant passes AND covenant assumption confirmed), debt is never guessed (src/gridq/finance/debt.py:96-114 returns None + a B001 warning when financing.debt_gearing_pct/rate/tenor keys are absent, which they are in both releases), the credit policy refuses non-monotonic mappings (finance/credit.py), and quote identity is timestamp-free and deterministic. The priced floor is structurally clean: _PRICED_FIRMNESS = {FIRM, CONTRACTED} (cashflows.py:350) and GATED_UPSIDE_STREAMS can never be FIRM. But for firming products specifically, the machinery is shaped for a merchant-arbitrage asset, not an availability-commitment asset, in four load-bearing ways: (1) P90 is one flat revenue haircut (15%, placeholder) applied identically to every stream and year with costs held flat (cashflows.py:457-478) — a contracted CM payment and merchant arbitrage get the same downside, and the actual risk of availability products (discrete penalty/shortfall events, bounded by the CM Rules' non-delivery charge caps) has no representation anywhere: grep for "penalt" in src/gridq returns zero hits, and ValueStreamResult.annual_cost_gbp (schemas/market.py:26) is never used for it. (2) Debt sizing is principal = capex × gearing measured against TOTAL P90 CFADS (debt.py:191-215, level annuity, sculpting documented as future work) — the contracted-vs-merchant split exists only as a display artifact (outputs/viewmodel.py:245 _split), never as a debt-sizing basis, whereas lenders size battery debt primarily against contracted CFADS at a lower covenant and heavily discount merchant. (3) The final-contract-year deliverability proof — the single thing that makes a CM/flex commitment underwriteable — is a permanent stub: end_of_life_deliverable is hardcoded None with a data-ask (sizer.py:214,285,301), the objective only rejects an explicit False nothing ever produces (objective.py:82), and nothing computes power × de-rating at year-N degraded capability from degradation.annual_capacity_fade_pct. (4) Firming revenue cannot currently even reach the floor: CM/Piclo sit permanently CONDITIONAL because no caller populates GateContext and the MILP never sets deliverable_capacity_mw/tender_window_discharge_mwh, and CONDITIONAL is excluded from the priced floor — correct honesty, but the producer side (evidence-bearing gate facts, held-SoE/window constraints in the solver) is entirely unbuilt.

On content, no firming quote can be bankable today by construction: 0 confirmed assumptions in either release; capacity_market.clearing_price_gbp_per_kw_yr (£30/kW/yr) and de_rating_factor_by_duration_h are needs_review awaiting NESO/EMR Delivery Body series (assumptions/gb_v1_0.yaml:192-220, docs/assumptions_to_source.md Tier 5 #18-19); local_flex_piclo.utilisation_price (£150/MWh) awaits per-DNO Piclo tender data (#20); dscr_covenants.min_p90_dscr 1.30 is explicitly "illustrative, not a confirmed lender term" (gb_v1_0.yaml:122-131); the debt keys are absent (Tier 1 #2, Sim's); and p50_p90_haircuts/degradation are uncalibrated placeholders awaiting M7/OEM data. Worse, the library has no availability-price key (Piclo/DSO products pay £/MW/h availability plus £/MWh utilisation per the ENA Open Networks standard flexibility agreement — only utilisation exists), no CM penalty-regime section (non-delivery charges are deterministic and bounded — 1/24 of monthly capacity payment per missed settlement period, capped at 200% of monthly and 100% of annual capacity revenue under the Capacity Market Rules / EMR Settlement) and no availability/outage assumption at all. And the two live releases have diverged: gb_v1_0 lacks financing.credit_rate_tenor_policy that gb_v0_1 carries, so the newer release cannot even resolve the credit mapping. Customer-side firming (fixed-bill/capped-bill) is furthest out: there is no bill model in v1 (BillSavingsView permanently pending, C017), and pricing.py's savings_share/capacity_fee are labelled structure-only placeholders — though the raw material (tariff calendar + HH load + solved dispatch) already exists in the MILP layer.

Verdict: the platform's discipline (DSCR unavailable rather than guessed; nothing bankable on placeholders) is exactly right and should not be softened. What the firming lens demands is new risk SHAPES, not looser gates: firmness-differentiated P90, a penalty cost line with its own assumption section, contracted-basis debt sizing, and a computed (not asserted) end-of-life deliverability verdict. All four fit the existing seams — per-stream revenue already lands in annual_revenue_by_stream_gbp with firmness on the record, DebtTerms/assess_debt accept injection, SizingGridPoint already carries end_of_life_deliverable, and dscr_covenants/p50_p90_haircuts are existing sections that can grow keys without schema surgery.
