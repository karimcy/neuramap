## F-001 | M3 dispatch | HIGH | FIXED (D31, 2026-07-02)
**Claim:** The dispatch MILP chains state-of-energy across missing intervals as if they were adjacent.
**Evidence:** `src/gridq/optimisation/milp_solver.py` — `_load_series` (L202) drops null readings by boolean mask and returns a compacted array; rows absent from the CSV are simply not present. `_solve_milp` builds the SoE recurrence `soe - soe.roll(t=1) - charge*(rte*dt) + discharge*dt == 0` (L300, L313) over a positional `RangeIndex`, so two readings separated by a data gap of hours or days are treated as consecutive half-hours: the battery's charge state teleports across the gap and the optimiser can exploit it (charge cheap before a gap, discharge into a red band after it, with no time passing).
**Spec:** goal directive: "segment MILP SoE at gaps, never chain across them". Manual ch09 position to be checked (drift either way).
**Fix (DONE):** D31 — predecessor-permutation SoE recurrence: segment at any timestamp delta != expected interval; per-segment cyclic wrap; stable timestamp sort with warning; MPC first segment anchored to initial_soe. Reduces exactly to the global cyclic constraint on gapless data (pinned golden hash unchanged). `src/gridq/optimisation/milp_solver.py`.
**Test (DONE):** `tests/test_goal_invariants_dispatch.py::test_soe_never_chains_across_gaps` — gapped flat-band series must cycle nothing (was 44,923 kWh/yr phantom discharge before the fix). Full MILP/MPC/golden/engine suites green after fix. Note: DST spring-forward label gap on naive local data currently creates one conservative extra segment boundary; physical-continuity recovery is part of F-003.

## F-002 | M4 sizing / X.1 assumptions | MEDIUM | FIXED (D35, 2026-07-03 — conservative-neighbour lookup)
**Claim:** Duration-keyed assumption lookup floors to the largest tabulated duration <= d, which is anti-conservative for warranty cycle caps.
**Evidence:** `src/gridq/optimisation/_assumptions.py::value_for_duration` (L46) — floor lookup. Warranty `max_cycles_per_year_by_duration_h` decreases with duration ('1' > '2' > '4' cycles), so a 3.0h candidate inherits the 2h cap: MORE cycles per year than either neighbouring warranty point would allow at 3h. For CM de-rating (factor increases with duration) flooring is conservative, so the same primitive is conservative on one table and anti-conservative on the other. Independently confirmed by the adoption review of karim/north-star-drive: their continuous `SizingDomain` evaluates off-key durations as the norm, the MILP throughput cap floors a 3h candidate to the 2h key (365 cycles = 1095 kWh/kW-yr vs 730 at 2h / 1000 at 4h — the cap RELAXES between keys), no test on either lane pins the direction, and a wide search will preferentially select just-below-key durations because they get the higher cap.
**Spec:** goal WS2 required "a labelled interpolation policy"; the adopted north-star D28 keeps conservative flooring and defers interpolation to sourced assumption content (X.1 door), reasoning from the CM table (where flooring IS conservative) — the decision text does not address the decreasing warranty table.
**Fix (DONE, D35):** the conservative-NEIGHBOUR lookup proposed in C030, taken as the resolving decision when the wide search went live (D34 made off-node durations the norm): warranty cycle caps take the CEILING node between tabulated points (`ceiling_value_for_duration`, applied in `milp_solver` + `mpc_solver` — a 3h candidate gets the 4h point's 250 cycles, never more than any bracketing contract point allows); CM de-rating keeps the floor (its conservative direction). No intermediate value is invented — north-star D28's no-interpolation stance is preserved; the earlier interpolation fix stays withdrawn. Above the largest node: largest node's value + a loud needs_review warning.
**Test (DONE):** `tests/test_goal_invariants_dispatch.py::test_duration_key_lookups_are_conservative_in_both_directions` (direction pinned on BOTH table shapes + node exactness + malformed tables) and `::test_off_node_duration_gets_the_ceiling_warranty_cap_in_dispatch` (a real 3h solve binds at the 4h contract point's cap, with the D35 warning on the result).

**Spec:** goal priority "DST/leap-year/timezone ... handling in the HH series".
**Fix (DONE):** `_parse_timestamps` now handles the three source classes deterministically: naive stays naive (documented as local-clock period-start), a uniform UTC offset stays tz-aware, and mixed offsets are re-parsed onto a single tz-aware UTC axis (no row dropped; unparseable still raises). `tariff_calendar._hours_weekday` converts a tz-aware index to Europe/London before reading hour/day-of-week (GB clock content), so a UTC-labelled summer 15:00 half-hour correctly lands in the 16:00 BST red band; naive input is unchanged. `milp_solver` gap segmentation verified tz-aware-safe: `.values` deltas are physical UTC instants, so a DST clock change is not a gap for tz-aware input; a naive spring-forward label jump keeps its one conservative segment boundary. NOT done (deliberately, minimal-change): DST-aware expected-interval counts for *naive* local series — a naive spring/fall day still counts 2 missing / 2 duplicate intervals; honest warnings, never silently wrong, and the tz-aware path has no phantom counts.
**Test (DONE):** `tests/test_goal_invariants_hh_tz.py` — `test_naive_local_spring_forward_red_band_on_local_label`, `test_naive_local_fall_back_red_band_on_local_label` (band by local label, solver OPTIMAL, segmentation only at the transitions), `test_utc_labelled_bands_match_naive_local_per_instant`, `test_summer_1500_utc_half_hour_is_in_the_red_band`, `test_mixed_offset_csv_round_trips_and_solves` (parses to UTC, zero phantom missing/duplicates at the clock change, solves OPTIMAL with no segmentation, red-band mask = GB local-clock expectation).

## F-013 | M3 dispatch / M4 sizing (diagnosis) | — | NOT A DEFECT — verdict recorded (2026-07-17, Olly, issue 018 slice A1)
**Claim (the meeting hypothesis):** the MILP "is only LP / charge-only, no discharging is taking place, which is wrong"; and if Robert Thomas returns a low/absent IRR, "most likely something is wrong with the revenue streams."
**Evidence (controlled experiment — identical tariff, load shape the only variable):** priced the demo cold store and Robert Thomas (Haywood Oaks) on the SAME illustrative red/amber/green tariff (0.30/0.18/0.13 + DUoS 0.13/0.02/0.005).
- The engine **does** discharge and is not charge-only: demo floor = £14,438/yr at 100 kW/2h, £49,456/yr at 189 kW/8h, with 142.99 MWh of red-band discharge (`optimisation/valuation.py` per-stream £).
- Robert Thomas floor = £2,915 / £4,187 / £5,307 per year at 50/100/200 kW·2h — ~3.4× lower than the demo at equal size, despite a HIGHER peak (421 vs 248 kW).
- Cause is load shape, computed from the real metered series (`examples/real/robert_thomas_haywood_oaks_HH.csv`, 12,096 HH, 100% complete, T1): only **6.4%** of consumption (9.5 MWh/yr) falls in the weekday 16:00–19:00 RED band; **68.2%** (101 MWh) sits overnight/weekend in the CHEAPEST green band. Discharge is capped behind-the-meter at `discharge_ub = min(power, load)` (`milp_solver.py:451`), so with ~0 load in the expensive window there is almost nothing to displace.
- `gridq run` on Robert Thomas returns `selection_status = no_viable_configuration`: 141 candidates solved and priced, every `project_irr_pct = null` (revenue below opex at every size ⇒ non-conventional cashflow ⇒ IRR unavailable) — the honest D39 verdict, correctly reached.
**Resolution (NOT A DEFECT in the floor engine):** the discharge≈0 appearance on overnight-heavy sites is load-coincidence physics, not a bug; the priced floor and per-stream valuation are correct. The real product gap is **coverage of the streams that would make such a site viable** — export/wholesale arbitrage (charge cheap overnight, sell into expensive periods, which requires an export decision variable RT does not have today: no-export floor), local flexibility, and capacity market — all currently gated/£0. Robert Thomas is a genuinely poor STANDALONE behind-the-meter arbitrage site; its viability case, if any, lives in those gated streams (issue 018 WS-A export, WS-B real prices, WS-C local flex) plus the unresolved intake question of WHY the load is overnight-heavy/midday-zero (existing PV vs night-rate operation — changes the model class; ranked data-ask, relates C028).
**Follow-ups:** issue 018 A2/A3 (consent-gated export + gated-upside valuation), B2/B3 (real HH price inputs + invoice tariff bands), C1–C3 (local flex net of aggregator cut); data-ask to James on the overnight profile. No dispatch-engine change is warranted by this finding.
